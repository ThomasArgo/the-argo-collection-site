Historical background

01The lion and the constitution

Belgium broke from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1830. The National Congress drew up a constitution in 1831, and Leopold swore his oath that July. These were recent events when this coin was struck. The tablet makes that constitutional beginning part of the reverse design.

The motto around it is short enough to read even on a small coin. Its promise of unity sits alongside the name of the monarch on the other face. One face shows a royal initial and crown. The other gives the lion a document to guard.

[1][3][4]

02Two hundredths of the new franc

The new state introduced the Belgian franc in 1832. The value on this copper piece was two hundredths of a franc. The monetary law and the constitution were different undertakings, but small change carried the results of both into daily life. The value under the lion was the useful part at a counter. The motto and tablet told people which state stood behind it.

[1][2]

03A date shared with the first railway

The year 1835 also saw the inauguration of Belgium’s Brussels–Mechelen railway. Leopold had supported the project since 1832. A railway and a decimal currency addressed different needs of the young state. On the coin itself, the connection is simply the year. The lion and tablet tell the constitutional story.

[4]

Identification notes

1835 and its varieties

The date is read as 1835. The catalog lists both wide- and narrow-rim pieces and distinguishes punctuation at the engraver’s signature. Wear along the lower reverse limits what can be read there, and separately framed photographs do not establish die alignment. The page therefore uses the KM family rather than a decimal subtype.
