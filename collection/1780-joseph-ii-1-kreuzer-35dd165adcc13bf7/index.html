Historical background

01The year Joseph took over

Joseph had been Holy Roman Emperor since 1765, and he shared government of the Habsburg lands with Maria Theresa. Her death in 1780 gave him the freedom to govern those territories alone. The date on this little coin straddles the transition. It supplies no month or evidence of which side of that change the piece was made.

The pace of reform quickened after 1780. The Tolerance Patent followed in 1781, easing restrictions on several non-Catholic communities. The court’s reforms dealt with religion, administration and much else. The copper coin’s purpose was simpler. It offered a recognizable unit that people could count out while those policies changed around them.

[3][4]

02Read the wreath first

A kreuzer was one sixtieth of a gulden in the system given for these types. Written out as EIN KREUTZER, the value fills the center. The mint letter is much smaller and sits where it can disappear into wear below the date. In these photos the distinction is obvious: the words that helped people spend it have survived much better than the mark that would help us place it.

[1][2]

Identification notes

Two types with the same date

The 1780 co-regency entry KM 2055 has a distinct obverse legend including titles of Germany and Jerusalem. The KM 2056 series names Hungary and Bohemia and includes several 1780 entries. The apparent BO and REX on this coin favor the latter comparison. A clearer rim and mintmark are needed for a firm entry or workshop. Neither catalog weight is a measurement of this coin.
