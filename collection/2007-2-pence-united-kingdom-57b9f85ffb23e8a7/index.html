Historical background

01The motto on the ribbons

The feathers are the badge associated with the Prince of Wales. The Royal Household traces its use to the fourteenth century and the time of Edward, the Black Prince. The German motto means I serve. On the coin one word sits on each side of the coronet, giving the feathers the centre of the design.

Christopher Ironside adapted the badge for the decimal two pence. The upright central plume balances the two that curl outward. The words TWO PENCE follow the rim, with the numeral underneath. The portrait on the other face still identifies Elizabeth II as the reigning monarch.

[1][4]

02A denomination made for decimal change

The two pence entered circulation on Decimal Day in 1971 alongside the halfpenny and penny. With a hundred pence to a pound, it was worth one fiftieth of the new pound. The original inscription NEW PENCE marked the change. The word TWO replaced NEW on this denomination in 1982, well before the date on this piece.

[2][5]

03The last years of the feathers

By 2007 the Rank-Broadley portrait had been in use for nearly a decade. The steel core was another established change by then. The 2008 shield redesign gave the two pence a different reverse, so this piece belongs near the end of the long feathers series. The shared portrait and coating make it a useful companion to the 2006 penny. The difference is in what each coin chose to show under the queen’s name.
