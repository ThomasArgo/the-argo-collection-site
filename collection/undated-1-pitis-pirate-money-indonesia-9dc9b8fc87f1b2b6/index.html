Historical background

01Comparing the holder attribution with other pitis

Palembang, on the Musi River in southern Sumatra, had local small change that included cast tin-alloy pitis. Some holed forms carry writing on one face and leave the other plain. That combination offers a starting point for this piece, whose raised ring around the hole remains much clearer than the field around it.

The illustrated numismatic study shows how much the pieces can differ, even among those whose inscriptions can be partly read. A round hole and plain back narrow the comparison, but several issues share those features. The strokes visible here do not establish which issue it belongs to.

[1][2][3]

02Small change in a trading region

The Palembang studies describe small local coins alongside other currencies used in regional trade. Pepper and tin linked the sultanate to outside merchants. The low-value pieces served a different purpose from the larger silver and gold currencies recorded in the region. That is the historical setting of the comparison coins.

Identification notes

What the ICG holder records

The photographed holder reads ND, 1 Pitis, ICG – Genuine, Indonesia-Palembang and Pirate Money. ICG Genuine is the designation on the holder. It supplies no numerical grade. The label uses Pirate Money as a nickname. The denomination is 1 Pitis.

The holder is direct evidence of ICG’s attribution. The visible features allow comparisons with published Palembang pitis, but the inscription is too worn to establish the exact issuer, production date or variety independently.

What could help identify it

Straight-on photographs with a scale, close-ups under low-angle light from two directions, and an edge view would help separate surface marks from possible lettering. A weight, outer diameter and hole diameter would give three more points of comparison with published pieces.
