Historical background

01Antlers on the quarter

Emanuel Hahn’s caribou appeared in 1937, when Canada replaced the crossed maple boughs on several denominations. The Mint’s account of the redesign notes that officials had considered the caribou for the five-cent piece and the schooner for the quarter. The familiar assignments were the result of a choice rather than an inevitable pairing of image and value.

Hahn let the antlers reach up and across the coin, giving the denomination room between their branches. The beaded border holds the country and date around a design that can be recognised from its outline. The quarter became a place for commemorative designs too, and the caribou gave those issues a familiar counterpart.

[2][4]

02Two marks in 2006

The Royal Canadian Mint struck ordinary caribou quarters as well as special designs in 2006. Its annual report describes the pink-ribbon and Medal of Bravery coins. The photographed piece has the ordinary caribou. Even within that design the date has two visible forms, one with P and one with the Mint logo below the portrait.

[1][2][5]

03The queen without a crown

Susanna Blunt’s portrait entered circulation in 2003. The simple neckline and uncovered hair make it easy to distinguish from Dora de Pédery-Hunt’s diademed profile on the 2001 five cents in this collection. The title still runs around the same part of the coin. The small P is separate from it and identifies the plated construction.

[1][3]

Identification notes

The mark under the bust

The mark reads P, and the reverse shows the 2006 caribou. That combination matches the cataloged plated issue. The P does not identify a mint city. The dimensions and alloy describe the type, and the exact finish remains open.
