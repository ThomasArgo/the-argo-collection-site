Historical background

01The shot remembered in Santo Domingo

On the night of 27 February 1844, supporters of separation from Haiti gathered in Santo Domingo. Dominican civic accounts remember Mella firing his blunderbuss at the Puerta de la Misericordia. It became the remembered signal of the independence action. The proclamation and the raising of the new flag are associated with the Puerta del Conde. The two places belong to the same night but record different parts of it.

The portrait shows Mella frontally in a coat, with his surname in small letters. Its familiarity comes in part from the central bank’s use of the same founder across different forms of money. The coin turns a face from the story of independence into part of ordinary transactions.

[3]

02When ten pesos became a coin

The central bank says it decided in 2004 to stop reprinting the ten-peso note and proceed with a bimetallic coin. Mella’s denomination completed the series alongside Juan Pablo Duarte on one peso and Francisco del Rosario Sánchez on five. The metals make the denominations visually distinct. On ten pesos the portrait fills a golden centre, while the five-peso pattern reverses that light-and-dark arrangement.

[1][2]

03A domestic issue with overseas production

The central bank issued the coin and put its name on the rim. A manufacturer could be located overseas. The Mint of Poland records a Dominican central-bank contract for five- and ten-peso circulation coins in 2009. The numismatic listing assigns the dated 2010 ten pesos to that mint. The evidence supports the attribution without a readable mint signature on this coin.

[1][2][4]

Identification notes

Two dates and the earlier wording

The year appears on both faces, with MELLA by the portrait and PESOS alone below the value. That combination matches KM 106. The article uses the dimensions and alloys of that type. The two flat photographs leave the edge and specimen measurements unchecked.
