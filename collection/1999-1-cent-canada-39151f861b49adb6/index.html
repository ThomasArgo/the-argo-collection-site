Historical background

01Copper on the outside

In 1997 the Royal Canadian Mint brought the round cent back and changed how it was made. The twelve-sided bronze coin had been 98 percent copper. Its replacement was mostly zinc, with a thin layer of copper applied by electroplating. A familiar colour and the same maple twig made the change less obvious than the new outline. The published weight dropped by a quarter of a gram.

The new core mattered to the Mint, while the amount mattered at the till. This piece still paid one cent. The light weight and copper surface belong to a period when much of the work of redesigning the penny was happening beneath the portrait and the leaves.

[1][2][4]

02A portrait made in Canada

Dora de Pédery-Hunt’s portrait first appeared on Canadian coins in 1990. The Mint describes it as the first effigy of a reigning monarch made by a Canadian artist. The 1999 portrait shows the queen with a diadem, earrings and necklace. The Latin title is the same as on the 1989 penny, but the jewellery and the way the bust meets the rim help tell the portraits apart.

Across the coin, Kruger Gray’s two leaves date back to 1937. The same cent combines a design from that earlier overhaul with a newer portrait and a different way of making the blank. A person receiving it in change had plenty of familiar detail to go by.

[2][3]

03When change stopped coming in pennies

The Mint struck the last cent in 2012 and stopped distributing them in 2013. Cash totals could then be rounded to the nearest five cents. The cent remained a unit for writing prices. This coin dates to a time when the difference between two adjacent cent amounts could still be handed over as a separate piece of metal.

[2][4]

Identification notes

The unmarked 1999 issue

The round shape, date and portrait agree with KM 289. Numista separately records a 1999 P-marked steel test piece. The area below the bust appears unmarked in the original photograph, supporting the usual zinc-core attribution. The composition is a type identification. This coin has not been weighed or magnet tested, and its strike finish remains open.
