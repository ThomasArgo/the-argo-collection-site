5 Zlotych

1996 · Republic of Poland · Narodowy Bank Polski

This five-złoty piece was struck while Poland was still changing over to a new scale of money. Its denomination equalled 50,000 old złotych. Above the date, a restored crown makes the eagle a reminder of a political change as well.

Historical background

01Dropping four zeroes

By the end of 1994 everyday prices and wages could run into millions of złotych. Under the redenomination law, 10,000 old złotych became one new złoty on 1 January 1995. A million became 100. Five of the new złotych therefore represented 50,000 in the old accounting. The conversion made numbers easier to read while preserving the value they represented at the point of conversion.

The old money remained in circulation through the end of 1996. During the changeover two people could quote a price with very different numbers and mean the same amount. This coin’s 1996 date puts it within that period. Its bimetallic construction and large figure gave the new denomination a distinct appearance.

[3][4]

02The crown above the eagle

The White Eagle long predated the new złoty. In the Polish People’s Republic the state emblem used an eagle without a crown. The crown returned after the political changes of 1989, and the revised emblem was set out in 1990. You can see the small crown at the top of the golden-colored center. Around it is the name of the Republic of Poland.

The reverse does something quite different. Ewa Tyc-Karpińska lets the numeral take up most of the coin, then uses strips of oak foliage and lines to carry the design across the join between the metals. The national emblem and the amount are each easy to find, even though the two faces have little in common visually.

[1][5]

03The top coin in the new series

Narodowy Bank Polski prepared nine denominations, from one grosz to five złotych, for the cash changeover. The first five-złoty pieces of this design carry the year 1994 although the denomination entered the new circulation in January 1995. The 1996 piece belongs to that same design. Its date marks a later striking of the type rather than the year the new unit was introduced.

[2][4]

Identification notes

Warsaw mark and fine lettering

The 1996 date, the small mark by the talon and both faces match the Warsaw issue. A Polish specialist catalog reports a 1996 die distinction involving the distance between the T in ZŁOTYCH and the inner ring. I’ve left that fine distinction open. The alloy and dimensions describe the type, and the edge is outside the views shown here.
