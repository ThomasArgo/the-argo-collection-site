Historical background

01The words around the crown

The archive folder says Baden, a reasonable place to start for a German-language kreuzer. The words around the eagle settle the question. Oesterreichische names Austria, and the initials K·K abbreviate imperial and royal. The Germanisches Nationalmuseum records the same inscription, shield and denomination on an Austrian example dated 1816.

Francis was the last Holy Roman Emperor as Francis II and the first emperor of Austria as Francis I. By 1816 the Holy Roman Empire had been dissolved for a decade. The Austrian title and imperial emblem belong to the monarchy he retained through the wars with Napoleon.

[1][2][3]

02A date that stayed on the dies

Scheidemünze names subsidiary money. Copper filled the small steps between prices and payments while paper and silver occupied other parts of the currency system. Austria had already devalued its paper money in 1811. The founding of its privileged national bank in 1816 came during the longer effort to restore order after the war.

The 1816 on the coin is a reliable reading of the design, and the catalog reports later manufacture with that date. The coin can therefore carry 1816 without telling us when this particular piece was struck.

[1][4][5]

Identification notes

Two ways to name the faces

The viewer follows the original filenames and the Numista convention, with the arms first. The Germanisches Nationalmuseum describes its denomination face first. Its example is marked B. That letter belongs to the museum coin. This specimen’s mint and edge have not been established from the supplied views.
